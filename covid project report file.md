## COVID-19 India Data Analysis and Visualization





#### Project Overview





This project analyzes the spread and impact of COVID-19 across Indian states and union territories using publicly available case and vaccination datasets. The objective is to clean, process, analyze, and visualize the data to identify national trends, state-wise variations, recovery patterns, mortality rates, and vaccination progress. The analysis covers India's first major COVID-19 wave and provides insights into how different states were affected during the pandemic.





#### Objectives





Collect and analyze COVID-19 case data for India.

Clean and preprocess raw datasets.

Study national and state-level infection trends.

Compare recovery and mortality rates across states.

Examine vaccination progress in India.

Generate meaningful visualizations and insights.

Summarize findings for decision-making and further research.

#### Key Findings





###### 1\. Dataset Coverage



The dataset covers:



35 Indian States and Union Territories

186 days of COVID-19 records

Period: 30 January 2020 to 6 August 2020



This corresponds to the initial phase of the pandemic and India's first major COVID-19 wave.



###### 2\. National COVID-19 Situation



By the end of the observation period:



Metric	Value

Confirmed Cases	\~1.96 Million

Recoveries	\~1.33 Million

Deaths	\~40,700

Recovery Rate	\~68%

Death Rate	\~2.1%



The daily case count continued to rise sharply, indicating that the first wave had not yet reached its peak.



###### 3\. State-wise Concentration



COVID-19 cases were heavily concentrated in a small number of states.



Major contributors included:



Maharashtra

Tamil Nadu

Andhra Pradesh

Karnataka

Delhi



Together, these states accounted for the majority of reported infections.



###### 4\. Recovery Rate Analysis



Recovery performance varied significantly across states.



Observations:



Delhi achieved a recovery rate close to 90%.

Karnataka had a substantially lower recovery percentage during the same period.

Differences likely reflect variations in outbreak timing and healthcare response.



###### 5\. Mortality Analysis



Death rates also differed across states.



Key observations:



Gujarat reported one of the highest mortality rates (\~3.8%).

Bihar and Telangana maintained mortality rates below 1%.

Differences may be influenced by demographics, healthcare capacity, and testing intensity.



###### 6\. Vaccination Progress



Although vaccination began after the analyzed case period, vaccination data provides context for India's subsequent response.



National vaccination achievements include:



More than 2.2 billion vaccine doses administered.

Over 950 million individuals fully vaccinated.



This demonstrates the scale of India's public health response following the first wave.

#### Conclusion



The analysis highlights the progression of COVID-19 during India's first wave and reveals significant regional differences in infection rates, recovery performance, and mortality outcomes. The findings show that the pandemic was concentrated in a handful of states, while recovery and death rates varied considerably across the country.



The vaccination data demonstrates the large-scale public health response that followed. Overall, the project showcases how data cleaning, exploratory data analysis, and visualization techniques can be used to derive meaningful insights from real-world public health datasets.
